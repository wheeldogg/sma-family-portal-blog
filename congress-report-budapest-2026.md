## 1. Conference Overview

I attended the 5th International Scientific Congress on SMA in Budapest (March 11–14, 2026) on behalf of our family's involvement with SMA Ireland. The congress was organised by SMA Europe and brought together clinicians, researchers, pharmaceutical companies (Roche, Biogen, Novartis, Scholar Rock), advocacy organisations, and SMA families from across Europe.

My primary focus was on the evolving evidence and clinical practice around combination therapy — specifically, add-on risdiplam for children who have already received Zolgensma — and on making connections that could support access efforts in Ireland.

## 2. Day-by-Day Summary

### Tuesday 11th March — Generics, Advocacy & European Models

The opening day featured a session on generic SMA treatments, highlighting that generic alternatives to branded risdiplam now exist internationally, with significant implications for global access. Christian Morris (SMA Europe) presented on the Italian SMA advocacy model — SMA Italy runs a comprehensive support system including a 24-hour helpline (Numero Verde Stella) staffed by physiotherapists, doctors, attorneys, geneticists, and peer counsellors. Their "team over the individual" approach and dedicated school support programme ("SMA: Equal But Different") are models Ireland could learn from. I also attended sessions on research sustainability, advocacy strategy, and the SMA Academy programme.

### Wednesday 12th March — Networking & UK Engagement

The morning included a presentation from the SMA UK team, who shared their survey report on the impact of disease-modifying therapies on patient outcomes and resource utilisation. I attended the MAPS Workshop on physiotherapy and activity, which covered FES (Functional Electrical Stimulation), Goals Attainment Scoring (GAS), adapted sports (iKarate, boccia), guitar as fine motor therapy, and finger lights for sensory play. The afternoon respiratory session covered cough assist protocols (Ambu bag technique, salbutamol as adjunct, 35+/35- pressure settings), oxygen at home, and the importance of having cough assist equipment in the car at all times. Significant networking happened throughout the day.

### Friday 14th March — Global Access, Emerging Science & Therapies

The morning opened with a session on global access to diagnosis, treatment, and care, including discussion of risdiplam's addition to the WHO Essential Medicines List and the Rainbow Fish Trial (presymptomatic risdiplam). This was followed by the NMR metabolomics session and the sensorimotor/fatigue research presentations. The afternoon therapies session featured Dr. Suzanne Quijano-Roy. I also connected with several international advocates regarding the Irish access situation and with Dr. Marta Gomez-Garcia de la Banda (APHP Paris), who offered to be CC'd on correspondence regarding French specialist consultation.

## 3. Dual Therapy: The Evidence Is Clear

The single biggest takeaway from Budapest is that **combination therapy for SMA is no longer theoretical — it is happening in clinical practice across Europe**, and the evidence supporting it continues to grow.

Key data points presented and discussed at the congress:

- **RESPOND trial** (Biogen, published in *Journal of Clinical Investigation*, Sept 2025): 95% of children who received nusinersen after Zolgensma showed motor improvements. NfL levels (a biomarker for neuronal injury) dropped 70–78%, confirming ongoing biological benefit.
- **Stanford presymptomatic cohort** (MDA 2025): All 8 infants who received Zolgensma within 2 months of birth — the best possible scenario — eventually required additional therapy. All improved after receiving it.
- **US multicenter case series** (MDA 2026): 19 patients across 6 centres received risdiplam after Zolgensma. All 14 assessed for motor function improved.
- **Systematic review** (Bemanalizadeh et al., *European Journal of Pediatrics*, 2025): 19 published studies and 6 ongoing trials. No additive toxicity signals in any combination.
- **Risdiplam is now on the WHO Essential Medicines List** — making it one of the most broadly endorsed treatments in the world.

## 4. European Practice vs Ireland

The gap between Ireland and the rest of Europe was a recurring theme throughout the congress:

- **France** has approved risdiplam as add-on therapy post-Zolgensma. French neuromuscular centres (FILNEMUS network) are actively prescribing dual therapy. I spoke with Dr. Suzanne Quijano-Roy and Dr. Marta Gomez-Garcia de la Banda (APHP Paris), both of whom confirmed this is standard practice for children showing plateau or suboptimal response.
- **Germany** is prescribing dual therapy on a case-by-case basis (confirmed directly in conversation with a Roche Medical Science Liaison at the congress).
- **Italy** has strong SMA advocacy infrastructure — their model of comprehensive family support was presented as a benchmark for other countries.
- **USA**: A 2023 Cure SMA community survey found 12.4% of US SMA patients are already on combination therapy in real-world practice, with 82% of families citing "wanting access to all possible treatments" as motivation.
- **Ireland's HSE MAP** currently restricts risdiplam to monotherapy — excluding children who received Zolgensma unless they meet "non-success" criteria (documented decline in motor scores or respiratory function). This framework was established before most of the current evidence existed and effectively penalises families whose children received the most expensive treatment available.

## 5. Roche Engagement

I spoke directly with a Roche MSL at the congress. Key points:

- Dual therapy is "the exception, not the rule" in Germany, but it is happening
- Roche is collecting real-world outcome data on combination therapy
- They committed to providing Ireland-specific information on risdiplam access
- Named Patient access may be possible through the treating consultant
- The conversation was constructive — Roche are aware of the Irish situation and did not dismiss the possibility of expanded access

## 7. Emerging Science

### NMR Metabolomics in SMA

New research is linking SMA to glucose and lipid metabolism — suggesting the disease's effects extend beyond motor neurons into whole-body metabolic function. This could provide new biomarkers beyond NfL for monitoring treatment response, help explain the fatigue and energy issues SMA patients experience daily, and eventually lead to metabolic interventions alongside existing treatments.

### Sensorimotor Function & Fatigue

The SMA Effort study and research into proximal predominant fatigue (PPF) are examining the lived experience of SMA beyond standard motor function scores. Many SMA patients report significant fatigue that impacts daily life but isn't captured by standard clinical assessments like CHOP-INTEND or HFMSE. This research validates what families already know: our children get tired in ways the clinical numbers don't always reflect.

### Rainbow Fish Trial

A Roche-sponsored study of risdiplam in presymptomatic infants identified through newborn screening. Directly relevant to Ireland as newborn screening for SMA is being rolled out. Combined with the Stanford data showing all presymptomatically-treated infants eventually needing add-on therapy, it strengthens the case for early and potentially combination treatment from the outset.

### Apitegromab (Scholar Rock) — A Fourth SMA Treatment

A selective anti-myostatin antibody — the first muscle-targeted SMA therapy, working through a completely different mechanism than existing SMN-enhancing treatments. The Phase 3 SAPPHIRE trial met its primary endpoint in October 2024. A US FDA filing is under review with launch anticipated in 2026, and European filing is underway. This could be added on top of any existing treatment to provide additional muscle-level benefit.

## 8. Practical Care Insights

Useful takeaways for all Irish SMA families:

- **Cough assist protocols:** Ambu bag technique, salbutamol as adjunct, 35+/35- pressure settings
- **FES (Functional Electrical Stimulation):** Worth discussing with physiotherapy teams
- **Guitar as therapy:** Fine motor exercise, especially for powered wheelchair users
- **iKarate:** Adapted martial arts programme — social + physical
- **Boccia:** Competitive sport option, great for inclusion
- **Finger lights:** Sensory and motor play tool for younger children

## 9. Recommendations for SMA Ireland

1. **Advocate for a review of the HSE MAP criteria** for risdiplam, specifically the exclusion of children who have received Zolgensma. The evidence base has changed significantly since these criteria were set.
2. **Support families pursuing dual therapy** through whatever access routes are available — Named Patient, EURO-NMD consultations, cross-border healthcare.
3. **Coordinate with SMA UK and SMA Europe** on dual therapy advocacy. This is not just an Irish problem — but Ireland is further behind than most.
4. **Share the practical care tips** from the conference with the wider Irish SMA community.
5. **Consider sending a representative to future SMA Europe congresses** — the networking alone was invaluable.
